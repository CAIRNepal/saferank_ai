# Reading notes (Phase 1)

| Paper | Read | Key facts | Effect on the plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rivera, Shah, Africa, Voudouris (2026) Item Response Theory for AI Safety, arXiv:2608.05086 | In full (main text and appendices) | 192 models, 8 benchmarks, 5,255 items (5,067 after preprocessing). Three factors: refusal strictness (bipolar, OR-Bench reversed), truthfulness, contextual harm. Regularised 2PL (log-normal prior on discrimination, normal prior on intercept). 1PL rejected on all benchmarks; 3PL helps AIC on one; BIC never prefers more than 2PL. Short-form protocol: 20 random 75/25 model splits, 100 random draws as baseline. Item-level MIRT compared by 5-fold held-out log-likelihood over models. One response per model-item pair, temperature 0, 1,024 tokens, via OpenRouter. | Make regularised 2PL primary. Adopt the 75/25 x 20 short-form protocol. Cite for the factor result and for the held-out-model validation idea. Pin the OpenRouter provider in Phase 6. |
| Ramprasath (2026) How Many Items Do You Really Need? (Stanford CS321M course report) | In full | Fantastic-Bugs data. BBQ: 42 models, 1,000 items. Median discrimination 1.12 (lowest of six). LogisticFM (K=2) beats 2PL by AIC (gap 0.267 per observation). BBQ "irreducible": Spearman rho never reaches 0.95 with max-information selection. Cross-benchmark theta correlations for BBQ below 0.5. Not peer reviewed. Reports discrimination values above 100 on some items, a sign of unregularised fits. | Direct prior evidence for H2 (multidimensional) and against H4 (short form). Novelty must rest on the full 58k-item bank, template-aware design, capability validity, DIF across model groups and uncertainty. Expect H4 to fail; report it honestly. |
| Parrish et al. (2022) BBQ, arXiv:2110.08193 | Metric definitions only | s_DIS = 2 * (biased / non-unknown) - 1. s_AMB = (1 - accuracy) * s_DIS. Nine social dimensions in the paper; 325 templates; over 58k items. | Formulas in the plan are correct. The released data has 11 category files and 343 (category, question_index) groups. |
| Habba et al. (2026) Growing Pains, arXiv:2604.12843 | Abstract and summaries | MIRT with anchor items and fixed-parameter calibration. 400+ models, 100 anchors per dataset, 2 to 3 points error, rho >= 0.9. Needs dozens of reference models (about 25 for homogeneous suites, about 100 for diverse suites). | Supports the Phase 6 design. The reference cohort of the main analysis should be at least 25 models. |
| Land and Bikel (2026) Auditing LLM Benchmarks with IRT, arXiv:2605.30504 | Main text, no appendix tables | 114 models, 7 preference and multiple-choice benchmarks. No BBQ. 4PL forced-ceiling mislabel indicator. | Not a BBQ scoop. Possible use: flag mislabelled BBQ items as an exploratory check. |
| Spagliardi et al. (2026) Efficient Safety Benchmarking via IRT, arXiv:2606.20626 | Not read | Adaptive testing on six safety benchmarks (as described by the AISI paper). | Read in Week 1. |
| Li et al. Adaptive Testing for LLM Evaluation, arXiv:2511.04689 | Not read | Listed in other papers' references. | Read in Week 1. |
| fl-IRT-ing with Psychometrics to Improve NLP Bias Measurement | Search snippets only | Conceptual proposal to apply IRT to bias benchmarks. Authors not verified. | Read in Week 1. Cite as prior conceptual work. |
| LLM Nominal Response Model, arXiv:2608.02966 | Search snippets only | Option-level psychometrics for multiple-choice benchmarks, with positional preference. | Consider a nominal-response sensitivity analysis. Read in Week 1. |
| Schroeders and Gnambs (2025) Sample size planning in IRT: a 10-decision framework, AMPPS 8(1) | Title, venue and citation only | A simulation-based planning framework (an R package, irtsim, implements it). Not a fixed rule of 100 models. | Replace the "about 100 models" justification with our parameter-recovery simulation. |
| Jodoin and Gierl (2001) | Cutoffs via secondary sources | Nagelkerke delta R2: A below 0.035, B 0.035 to 0.07, C 0.07 or more. | Plan cutoffs are correct. |
